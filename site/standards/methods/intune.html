Intune and host management method

methods/intune.md · Edition b0875cd of avd-assess, 2026-10-28. What changed

How to judge the way an estate manages its session hosts and the client devices that reach them: Intune for hosts and devices, against Group Policy where an estate keeps it. It is the assessment method applied to the Intune and host management pillar (intune in dimensions.yaml). Microsoft documents what Intune supports on AVD hosts, so the method links out to those pages and adds only the scope page ([G1]): which state to read, how devices map to pools, and which limits make a setting not applicable rather than missing.

Method

  1. Ask how hosts are managed before reading anything: Intune, Group Policy, both, or co-management; how hosts get Windows updates; and what client devices must prove to connect ([G2], [1, E01.03]).
  2. Check enrollment against the prerequisites: a host pool deployed through Azure Resource Manager in Intune's tenant, agent 1.0.2944.1400 or later, one MDM provider, Entra join with enrollment or hybrid join with device credentials, no Entra Domain Services join, and an image captured before enrollment. Reader sees the enrollment request; whether hosts are managed needs an Intune read, and an enrolled host isn't yet a managed one ([2, enrollment-prerequisites], [2, device-credentials], [2, one-mdm-provider], [2, no-entra-ds], [2, no-cloned-enrolled-image], [O1]).
  3. Check configuration delivery: settings catalog policies filtered to the multi-session edition, device settings to device groups and user settings to user groups, administrative templates through the catalog ([2, settings-catalog-only], [2, device-and-user-scope], [2, admx-supported]).
  4. Where Group Policy stays, check for one owner per setting: the GPOs on the hosts' OU reviewed, and no setting through both channels unless MDMWinsOverGP covers it. Group Policy wins by default ([1, D01.03], [3, default-gp-wins], [3, policy-csp-only], [4, mdm-support-percentage]).
  5. Check the security baseline delivered against the host baseline profile: the version for the hosts' Windows release, never a preview, with recorded departures ([2, baselines-available], [5, versioned-per-release], [5, no-preview-in-production], [G3]).
  6. Check host compliance: a device-targeted policy using the settings multi-session supports, and the tenant set so a device with no policy is noncompliant, because the default counts it compliant ([2, compliance-settings-supported], [2, compliance-device-targeted], [6, no-policy-default-compliant], [7, secure-by-default]).
  7. Check what client devices must prove: Intune compliance for managed devices and app protection for unmanaged phones, tablets and the browser, both through Conditional Access, which the identity pillar judges ([8, compliance-signal], [9, app-protection-for-clients], [1, G04.01]).
  8. Check apps and updates against what multi-session supports: apps in system context, targeted to devices, Required; no update rings or Autopatch on multi-session, where the image carries updates and Intune only sets quality update behavior; Autopatch on personal single-session hosts only ([2, apps-system-required], [2, quality-updates-catalog-only], [10, personal-only], [11, multisession-image-based], [1, E01.09]).
  9. Check the device lifecycle: no wipe, retire or lock on multi-session hosts, and the step that removes a host removes its Entra ID and Intune records ([2, remote-actions-unsupported], [2, orphaned-records], [O2]).
  10. Check the one profile setting Intune depends on: identity tokens don't roam ([2, no-token-roaming], [12, roamidentity-off]).
  11. Score what multi-session can't receive as not applicable, never as a gap, and record what stays unknown and why: no Intune read, no host read, or no operator answer ([G1]).

Not yet known

  • Whether matching Intune devices to host pools by name holds on a real estate, including hosts renamed or rebuilt under the same name. A sandbox run with enrolled hosts settles it; it waits on a cost estimate and the owner's go ([G1]).
  • How long Intune's own cleanup takes to remove records of deleted multi-session hosts; Microsoft gives no guarantee ([2, orphaned-records]).

Our observations

What our own runs showed about a cited claim. Evidence links are public and pinned.

  1. [O1]extends intune-enrolled-policies-left-to-owner, on avd-management#intune-recommended, 2026-10-21. The landing zone enrolls its Entra joined hosts in Intune by default (the Entra login extension carries Intune's MDM ID) but assigns no configuration, compliance, baseline or update policy; its scope page leaves those to the organization. An enrolled host is therefore not yet a managed one, and under the tenant's default compliance setting it counts as compliant with no policy at all. Enrollment and policy are separate rows for that reason.
  2. [O2]agrees device-records-outlive-hosts, on intune-avd-multisession#orphaned-records, 2026-10-21. Deleting the landing zone's demo hosts left their Entra ID and Intune device records behind until the cleanup script deleted them through Graph; a cleanup whose Graph sign-in timed out couldn't find the names on a second run until the fix kept the deployment record. Anything that recreates hosts (session host update, a rebuild) leaves the same records unless a step removes them.
